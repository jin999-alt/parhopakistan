STUDENT STUDY GUIDE

How to prepare for board exams

A practical approach to planning, practice and revision for Classes 9–12.

Home / Study Guide

Preparing for board examinations can feel difficult when many chapters and subjects need attention at the same time. A clear routine makes the work easier to manage. The steps below are general study suggestions; adapt them to your subjects, learning needs and the latest instructions from your school and examination board.

1. Start with the current syllabus

Before making a timetable, collect the current syllabus, textbook, class notes and any instructions shared by your teacher. Make a checklist of chapters and topics. Mark each topic as: understood, needs practice, or not yet revised. This gives you a realistic picture of the work ahead and helps prevent spending all your time on only one subject.

2. Break large chapters into smaller tasks

Instead of writing “study Physics” or “revise English” on a timetable, choose a specific task. For example: review one concept, solve five numerical questions, learn a set of definitions, practise a writing format or answer a group of short questions. Small tasks are easier to start and easier to check when completed.

3. Use active recall

After reading a topic, close the book and try to explain the main idea in your own words. Write down important points from memory, solve a question without looking at the worked example, or ask someone to test you. Then compare your answer with the textbook or class notes and correct gaps. Recalling information is more useful than repeatedly reading the same page without testing yourself.

4. Practise questions and show your working

Use textbook exercises and suitable practice papers to apply what you have learned. For numerical or problem-solving subjects, write each step clearly instead of jumping straight to an answer. For language and theory subjects, practise structuring a complete response and answering what the question actually asks. Follow any subject-specific method taught by your teacher.

5. Use model papers at the right time

A model paper is most helpful after you have studied the relevant material. First check its class, subject, board and academic session. Then attempt it under quiet, timed conditions. Do not look at solutions while attempting the paper. When finished, identify which questions were incorrect, incomplete or took too long. A model paper is a practice resource; it is not a promise about the next examination.

6. Keep an error notebook

For each mistake, record the topic, what went wrong and the correction. Common categories include: concept not understood, formula or definition forgotten, calculation error, question misunderstood, answer not explained clearly, or time management problem. Review this notebook every few days. Repeating the corrected question later helps you check whether the problem has actually been fixed.

7. Build a balanced weekly routine

Choose study periods that fit your school day and energy. A simple session can include a short review, one focused task, a brief break and a few questions from memory. Rotate subjects through the week so that difficult topics are revisited regularly. Keep time for sleep, meals, movement and rest; an exhausted student often finds it harder to concentrate.

Example seven-day revision cycle

  1. Day 1: list chapters and set achievable goals for the week.
  2. Day 2: revise concepts and textbook examples in two subjects.
  3. Day 3: practise written answers, definitions or numerical problems.
  4. Day 4: revisit difficult topics and test yourself without notes.
  5. Day 5: attempt a timed section from a suitable practice paper.
  6. Day 6: check the attempt, update your error notebook and revise weak areas.
  7. Day 7: review progress, prepare next week's checklist and take a proper break.

This is only an example. Adjust the number of subjects and tasks to your own timetable and examination date.

8. Prepare calmly for the examination day

In the days before an examination, focus on reviewing key concepts, practising a few questions and organising what you need. Confirm the date, reporting time, centre instructions and permitted materials from official sources. Avoid relying on rumours about “sure questions”. Try to maintain a regular sleep routine and arrive with enough time.

Remember: Your textbook, current syllabus and teacher's guidance remain the main reference points. The model-paper PDFs on ParhoPakistan's resources page are optional practice material, not a substitute for them.
